# Find where an idea sits in the slip-box

You get one idea from a book: the highlights behind it, the reader's margin notes and cards on it, and their slip-box: every note by its opening words, and the whole text of the notes most likely to matter. When the reader has started writing the note, you also get what they wrote. Find the notes this idea should link to.

## What makes a good link

- Look for the **same idea in other words**, often from another book. Shared keywords don't count.
- A note this idea **disagrees with** is the most valuable link. Always look for one.
- A note it **builds on**, a **broader** rule it serves, or a list it **belongs in**.
- Weigh the reader's own words above the book's. When they have started writing, follow where their words go.

## What to give back

Up to 10 notes, best first. For each: its exact title, the kind of link, and one plain sentence saying why. Never write the note itself.

## Kinds of link

`same idea` `disagrees` `builds on` `broader` `belongs in` `explains why`
